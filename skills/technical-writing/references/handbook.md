# Mechanics and commonly confused words

Use this reference in the final pass, after checking meaning and structure. Follow the target publication's style for punctuation, number formatting, spelling and capitalization.

- Join independent clauses with an appropriate conjunction, semicolon or sentence break. A comma alone often creates a splice. Fragments can work as table cells or list items; check complete prose separately.
- Use a colon after a complete lead-in. Keep punctuation from separating a subject from its verb. Set off nonrestrictive qualifications without accidentally changing their scope.
- Resolve ambiguous pronouns. In "The importer sends the file to the checker before it closes," name what closes.
- Keep modifiers near their subjects. Hyphenation can disambiguate compound modifiers; avoid automatically hyphenating every adjective pair. Do not hyphenate an ordinary adverb ending in -ly with its adjective.
- Follow venue conventions for dashes, quotation marks and possessives. Avoid changing mathematical minus signs, ranges or identifiers during a prose punctuation pass.
- Keep values and units consistent. Use a leading zero for ordinary decimal fractions, suitable spacing between values and units, and the required typesetting for percentages and ranges. Verify units rather than only formatting them.
- Define symbols and necessary acronyms at first meaningful use. A standalone abstract may need its own definitions. Punctuate displayed equations as part of sentences and resolve equation, figure, table and section references.

| Pair | Distinction to check |
|---|---|
| affect / effect | Influence versus result; effect can also mean bringing something about. |
| fewer / less | Countable items versus a mass quantity. |
| number / amount | Countable records versus an uncountable quantity. |
| imply / infer | What the author suggests versus what the reader concludes. |
| principal / principle | Main or a responsible person versus a rule. |
| criterion / criteria | Singular versus plural. |
| respectively | Two explicitly ordered lists must correspond. |
| its / it's | Possessive versus contraction. |

Do not treat every style preference as a grammar error. In particular, accepted usage for "data," restrictive "which" and contractions varies by field and publication. Preserve established scientific terminology and the authorized English variety.
