# Planning, drafting and revision

Begin with the reader's expertise, reason for reading and decision. A specialist checking reproducibility needs different detail from a manager deciding whether to fund further work. Do not assume either reader remembers previous discussions.

Write a purpose statement, then list the questions the document must answer. For a comparison these often include the problem, alternatives, evaluation conditions, observed effect, uncertainty, cost and limitations. Map questions to an outline. Confirm material changes in scope through the existing workflow; ordinary assigned writing needs no new administrative gate.

Generate ideas before ranking them: list, sketch or talk through candidate arguments. Group related points and choose an order that fits the material. Useful orders include time, physical arrangement, categories, parts of a whole, overview to detail, priority, problem to solution and cause to effect. Do not force every document into the same number of sections.

Draft from the outline. Start with a section whose evidence is available; leave visible working placeholders for unresolved facts rather than guessing. Keep a claim/evidence map and agreed terminology when several authors contribute. One editor reconciles notation and voice without silently rewriting another owner's technical assumptions.

Revise at different levels:

1. Completeness and accuracy: coverage, actual evidence, conditions, unfavorable cases and reproducibility.
2. Structure and emphasis: order, headings, contribution scope and placement of essential qualifications.
3. Paragraphs: a clear point, relevant support and explicit links between ideas.
4. Sentences and words: agents/actions, modifiers, technical meaning, economy and grammar.
5. Graphics and mechanics: captions, readable units, references, notation and final proofreading.

A break between passes, reading aloud or a reader unfamiliar with the draft can expose gaps. These are useful techniques, not mandatory waiting periods. Record a technical uncertainty for its owner; a style reviewer should not resolve it by changing the claim.
