# Reviewer responses and other technical documents

## Reviewer response

Preserve each reviewer's comment accurately and identify it by the required numbering. Address the specific concern before giving background. If accepting it, explain the change and its verified section/page/line or equation location. If disagreeing, explain the reason and supporting evidence courteously. Check that location references refer to the submitted revision.

Do not claim to have run an experiment, corrected a figure or added text until the artifact exists and was checked. A partial response should name what remains unresolved. Keep each answer understandable on its own, distinguish new evidence from a clarification of old evidence, and preserve qualifications. Do not promise resources or change the study scope without the project's authorization.

Synthetic shape:

> Comment 2: Please clarify the exclusion rule.
>
> Response: We clarified the rule in Section 3, paragraph 2 of the revised draft. Records missing the required identifier were excluded before evaluation. The revision now reports their count and the effect on the stated scope.

Use that wording only when those revisions and facts actually exist.

## Cover letter and technical email

State the document, purpose and requested action early. Give enough context to act, identify the intended attachment/revision and name any real deadline. For a draft review, ask focused questions rather than requesting undefined feedback. Drafting a message does not authorize sending it.

## Proposal and progress report

A proposal connects a problem to a concrete method, deliverable, resource estimate, schedule, checkpoints and risks. Estimates remain estimates. A progress report compares actual work with the plan, reports unfavorable findings or delays promptly, and proposes a feasible next step. Separate completed, running and planned work.

## Minutes, descriptions and procedures

Minutes record decisions, owners, dates and next actions rather than a transcript. A technical description explains the parts or stages in an order that makes their interaction understandable. A procedure specifies prerequisites, ordered actions, expected checks and relevant precautions before the affected step. Test a procedure against the actual environment instead of inventing tool flags.
